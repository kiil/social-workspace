# Community and Belonging

A community is a group that would notice if you stopped showing up. It is the most robust social structure there is: it supplies friends, introductions, status and a sense of place without needing you to start every connection yourself.

## Belonging versus contributing

There are two ways into a community, and the worked examples in [venues](../venues/index.md) show one of each:

- **Belonging** — becoming a regular somewhere: the same café, gallery, gym or park run, until people recognise you. Low commitment, slow, and pleasant. See the [art museum example](../venues/harbour-art-museum.md).
- **Contributing** — taking a role: volunteer, coach, organise, teach, sit on the board. Faster and deeper, because a role gives you a reason to talk to everyone and others a reason to trust you. See the [sailing club example](../venues/westbay-sailing-club.md).

Contributing works harder. If you have the time for only one, take a small role somewhere you already like being.

## Choosing a community

- Shares something you care about — an interest, a value, a place
- Meets regularly, ideally weekly
- Has room for newcomers and a way in: an intro session, a volunteer rota, a beginners' group
- Mixes ages and backgrounds, so it is not only a dating pool or only a work network

## Getting in

- Show up consistently for two or three months before judging it.
- Learn names and use them. Introduce newcomers to each other once you are no longer one.
- Say yes to the small jobs: setting up chairs, washing cups, running the sign-up sheet. They are how strangers become regulars.
- Join the informal part — the drink afterwards, the walk to the station.

## Watch-outs

- A community you are embedded in is expensive to leave. Be careful with romance inside it, and read [Boundaries and Consent](boundaries-consent.md) with that in mind.
- Roles can become a hiding place. Being useful is comfortable; it can stand in for actually getting to know anyone. Step out of the role regularly.
- Cliques exist. If after a fair try you are still outside, the problem may be the group, not you.
