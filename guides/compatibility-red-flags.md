# Compatibility Assessment and Red Flags

Evaluating fit and recognizing warning signs early. Warning signs come in tiers: **yellow flags** warrant a conversation and closer attention, while **red flags** and **deal-breakers** are grounds to slow down or walk away. When flags concern your physical or financial safety, treat [Safety and Vetting](safety-vetting.md) as the priority.

One caution about using this note: a vocabulary for disqualifying people is exactly what pre-emptive self-sabotage borrows to make an escape sound like judgment. If your flag-spotting sharpens specifically once someone becomes available and interested, read [Fear of Success and Pre-emptive Self-Sabotage](fear-of-success-intimacy.md) before you act on it. The tests there separate a real mismatch from an engineered one.

## Compatibility Indicators

- Shared core values (not necessarily interests)
- Similar relationship goals and timelines
- Compatible communication styles and conflict approaches
- Aligned lifestyle preferences (social, adventurous, domestic)
- Mutual respect for each other's boundaries and autonomy

## Green Flags

- They're considerate of your time and feelings
- They follow through on commitments
- They take responsibility for their mistakes
- They're genuinely interested in who you are
- They introduce you to friends or include you in their life
- They're open to feedback and willing to work on issues
- They accept a "no" easily and respect your [boundaries](boundaries-consent.md)

## Yellow Flags (Slow Down and Watch)

These aren't automatic disqualifiers, but they warrant attention and often a direct conversation:

- Occasionally slow or inconsistent with replies and plans
- Guarded or vague about parts of their life early on
- Mismatched pace — moving notably faster or slower than you
- Mild defensiveness when given feedback
- Complains about exes, though without a clear pattern of blame
- Habits or lifestyle differences that may or may not matter over time

## Red Flags (Serious Concern)

A pattern of these signals is grounds to step back and reassess:

- Consistently inconsistent or flaky — a pattern, not a one-off
- Disappears for days without explanation
- Makes excuses rather than taking responsibility
- Badmouths all their exes or has a recurring pattern of conflicts
- Pressures you for intimacy or commitment early
- Evasive about their life, finances, or past when trust is warranted
- Controlling, critical, or dismissive of your feelings

## Deal-Breakers (Grounds to End It)

- Non-negotiable values conflicts (family, religion, lifestyle)
- Any pushing of your boundaries after you've stated them
- Active addiction or substance abuse they're unwilling to address
- Dishonesty about fidelity, availability, or major life facts
- Commitment to other relationships, or unwillingness to commit at all
- Disrespect or cruelty toward you or those you care about
- Any behavior covered in [Safety and Vetting](safety-vetting.md) — these override everything

## Assessing Over Time

- How do they handle stress or disappointment?
- Do they prioritize you or consistently put others first?
- How do they speak about you to others?
- Do you feel energized or drained by them?
- Are they growing and working on themselves?
- Do they respect your autonomy or try to control you?
