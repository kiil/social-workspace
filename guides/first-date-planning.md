# First Date Planning and Execution

Setting up successful initial meetings that build attraction and connection. For a first meeting with someone new, read [Safety and Vetting](safety-vetting.md) first — the precautions there (public place, own transport, telling a friend) come before everything below.

## Venue Selection

- Choosing places conducive to conversation (avoid loud bars)
- Picking neutral territory where neither person has home-field advantage
- Considering activities that provide natural talking points
- Ensuring the venue matches both people's comfort levels
- Having a backup plan in case the original idea doesn't work

## Timing and Logistics

- Scheduling at a time when both people can be present and relaxed
- Keeping the first date relatively short (1-2 hours)
- Meeting in a public place for safety and comfort
- Arriving early or on time to set a positive tone
- Having a clear way to get there without logistics stress

## During the Date

- Being genuinely present and putting away your phone
- Asking open-ended questions and listening to answers
- Sharing about yourself without dominating the conversation
- Noticing what creates energy and what feels flat
- Reading their comfort level with pacing and physical proximity

## The Vibe Assessment

- Noticing whether there's natural chemistry and ease
- Paying attention to how they treat service staff and others
- Observing alignment in values and sense of humor
- Feeling whether you want to spend more time with them
- Recognizing if there's mutual interest, not just politeness
- Being careful with "I just don't feel it" when it arrives *after* they made their interest plain — that timing is the tell for [Fear of Success and Pre-emptive Self-Sabotage](fear-of-success-intimacy.md), and a good date is precisely when it fires

## Next Steps

- Following up within 24 hours if there was mutual interest
- Being direct about wanting another date rather than playing games
- Proposing a specific time and activity
- Accepting a no gracefully if interest isn't there
- Not over-investing emotionally before a second date confirms mutual interest
