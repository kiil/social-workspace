# Building Friendships as an Adult

Adult friendships rarely happen by accident. School and early jobs supplied the two ingredients friendship runs on — repeated, unplanned contact and shared time with no agenda — and adult life quietly removes both. This note is about putting them back on purpose.

## Why it is harder now

- **No built-in repetition.** Friendship mostly grows from seeing the same people again and again. Adults have to choose that repetition rather than receive it.
- **Everyone is busy, and everyone assumes everyone else is full.** Most people would welcome a new friend and are waiting for someone else to go first.
- **The awkward middle.** Between "we chatted once" and "we're friends" sits a stretch where someone has to propose a plan. That is where most potential friendships die.

## The ingredients

- **Recurrence** — pick one or two contexts you return to weekly: a club, a class, a team, a regular café, a volunteering shift. See [Venues and Contexts](venues-contexts.md).
- **Time** — friendships deepen with hours spent together, and casual time counts. Many small meetings beat one big one.
- **Initiative** — be the person who suggests the coffee, the walk, the next session. The skills in [Direct Contact Initiation and Assertiveness](direct-contact-initiation.md) apply unchanged; only the ask differs.
- **Disclosure, gradually** — share a bit more than small talk, match what comes back, and let it escalate in turns. [Communication and Emotional Intimacy](communication-intimacy.md) describes the same ladder for romance.

## From acquaintance to friend

- **Propose something specific.** "We should hang out sometime" means nothing. "I'm going to the Thursday market — want to come?" is a plan.
- **Make the first plan small and easy to leave.** A coffee, a walk, an event you were going to anyway.
- **Follow up within a week.** A short message referring to something they said keeps the thread alive.
- **Invite twice before you conclude anything.** One no is usually logistics. Two declines without a counter-offer is an answer; accept it cleanly.
- **Host.** A small, low-effort gathering — soup, a board game, a walk with three people — turns separate acquaintances into a group, and groups sustain themselves.

## Keeping friends

- Keep a light rhythm: a message, a shared link, a standing date.
- Remember what matters to them and ask about it next time. A line in their [people](../people/index.md) note is enough.
- Show up for the unglamorous moments — the move, the hospital visit, the bad week.
- Let some friendships be seasonal. Fading is not failure.

## Tracking it

Record new acquaintances in `people/` with how you met and the next step, and review them weekly. The point is not a CRM of friends; it is to stop good encounters from evaporating because nobody followed up.
