# Professional Networking Without the Cringe

Networking is making friends with people in your field, slowly, before you need anything from them. Treated that way, it uses exactly the same skills as the rest of this framework and loses most of its awkwardness.

## Principles

- **Give before you ask.** Share a useful link, make an introduction, answer a question. A network is built from small favours that run in both directions.
- **Curiosity beats pitching.** Ask what someone is working on and what is hard about it. People remember who was interested in them, not who presented well.
- **Depth over reach.** Twenty people who would take your call are worth more than five hundred connections.
- **Follow-up is the whole game.** A conversation at an event is worth nothing without a message within a few days.

## At events

- Arrive early, when groups have not formed yet and everyone is looking for someone to talk to.
- Use the context as the opener: the talk, the venue, the coffee queue. See [Opening the Conversation](opening-conversation.md).
- Join groups of three or more that stand open; avoid closed pairs. [Offline Signals](nonverbal-signals.md) applies directly.
- Leave cleanly: "Good to meet you — I'll send you that article." Then send it.

## Between events

- Keep a note per person in `people/` with context, what they care about, and a next step.
- Reach out when you have something relevant, not on a schedule.
- Ask for advice rather than favours. Advice invites people in; favours put them on the spot.
- Host something small: a lunch for four people who should know each other.

## Boundaries

- Mixing romance and professional contexts is high-risk; the costs of a misread are highest at work.
- You don't owe anyone your time because they are senior or useful.
- Keep confidential things out of shared notes.
